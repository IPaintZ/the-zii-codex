Cookfire · Workflow rework · mockup

Cook the batch,
then work the counter

Cooks don't make a stew per customer — they precook a larder of food, then sell it. So Cookfire splits in two: a Kitchen that turns ingredients into finished-goods stock, and a Counter that sells that stock. Buyback moves to the counter as a trade-in — ingredients for a discount, straight into your pantry, valued by how badly you need them.

03

How the trade-in rate is set

One rule covers both selling pre-made food and cooking to order — the only thing that changes is what counts as a "need."

High rate for the portion that meets a real need; low rate for the excess.

Made-to-order still cuts cost

Out of stock and the buyer wants it fresh? You cook it now — and the ingredients they brought go straight into the pot, so they knock that dish's cost down at the restock rate.

The prep chain still applies here too (bring Milk toward a Cheese Wheel) — decided earlier, unchanged.

The "target" is opt-in

Tiering needs to know short-vs-flush, which means a target (par) stock per ingredient. To skip busywork: leave it blank and that ingredient just uses the flat restock rate — no tiering.

Set a target only on the few that matter (beef, milk). It lives beside stock and the buyback-eligible flag in the Pantry tab.

04

Where this leaves the build

The decisions still stand — this rework just relocates them.

  • Kitchen / Counter tabs over shared catalog, pantry, buyers — plus a new finished-goods stock count per dish.
  • Buyback → trade-in at the counter; every ingredient lands in the pantry (your call). Rate keyed to stock vs. target, replacing the old recipe-need anchor.
  • Two rates (restock / overstock) and the per-buyer cascade carry over. Made-to-order keeps the cost-cutting and the prep-chain tracing.
  • Per-ingredient buyback-eligible flag and optional target both live in the Pantry tab.
